# FSIS Guidance for Sous Vide Cooking of Meat Products

## Source and scope

This document summarizes the relevant provisions of the **USDA Food
Safety and Inspection Service (FSIS), *Cooking Guideline for Meat and
Poultry Products (Revised Appendix A)*, December 2021**.

-   **Source:** [FSIS Cooking Guideline for Meat and Poultry Products
    (PDF)](https://www.fsis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/media_file/2021-12/FSIS-GD-2021-14.pdf)
-   **Relevant sections:** pages 18--20, 23--27, 31--38, 57--59, and
    66--79.
-   **Important scope note:** The guideline is intended primarily to
    support meat and poultry establishments' food-safety and HACCP
    systems. It is guidance, not itself a regulation; the document
    states that it does not have the force and effect of law. It should
    not be treated as a complete household sous vide safety code.

This summary follows the cited FSIS document. It distinguishes the
guideline's stated requirements and recommendations from practical
interpretation.

## 1. How FSIS treats sous vide and cook-in-bag processes

FSIS identifies two situations in which relative humidity does not need
to be addressed as a critical operating parameter:

1.  Moisture is inherently maintained around the product.
2.  The product is cooked using direct heat.

Under **"Situations when Humidity is Not Needed"** (page 31), FSIS gives
the following examples of processes in which moisture is inherently
maintained:

-   Completely immersing the meat or poultry product in a liquid cooking
    medium throughout the entire cooking process (for example, unbagged
    product cooked in water).
-   Cooking the product in a **sealed, moisture-impermeable bag**, such
    as cook-in-bag meat or poultry.

A typical sous vide process in which meat is sealed in an intact,
moisture-impermeable bag and kept immersed in a water bath throughout
cooking fits the second example.

**What this means:** When the process genuinely meets this condition,
the establishment does not need to monitor oven or ambient relative
humidity under the FSIS humidity options for that cooking process.

**What it does not mean:** The humidity exemption does not remove the
need to achieve an adequate pathogen lethality treatment, control the
heating process, or address post-cooking handling and storage. The bag
must remain sealed and contain the product's moisture.

FSIS also notes that cook-in-bag products may be eligible to be labeled
"pasteurized" when the applicable conditions are met (page 31; see
Attachment A3, page 81). This is not an automatic claim for every home
sous vide preparation.

## 2. Meat time-temperature combinations

FSIS Table 2, **"Time-Temperature Combinations for Meat Products to
Achieve Lethality"** (page 35), lists internal product temperatures and
the corresponding dwell times for:

-   **6.5-log lethality**
-   **7-log lethality**

The temperatures are the **minimum internal temperatures that must be
met in all parts of the meat product** for the total dwell time shown.
Both the time and temperature parameters must be met.

**Interpretation notes:**

-   The dwell time is the time at the required internal product
    temperature; it is not the total time the sous vide bath is running.
-   The table refers to the temperature in **all parts** of the product,
    not merely the water-bath setpoint or the surface temperature.
-   The footnote to Table 2 states that the required log reductions are
    achieved instantly (0 seconds) when the internal temperature of a
    cooked meat product reaches **158°F (70°C) or above**.
-   The table's footnotes also identify relative humidity and heating
    come-up time (CUT) as critical operating parameters when this table
    is used as the scientific support. The humidity exemption for
    qualifying cook-in-bag processes is described separately on page 31.

These are FSIS process-support combinations, not a guarantee that a
particular home cooking schedule is safe regardless of product
thickness, starting temperature, equipment performance, packaging
integrity, or handling.

## 3. What log reduction means

A log reduction describes a reduction in the number of viable organisms:

    Reduction   Approximate reduction in organisms
  ----------- ------------------------------------
        1-log                                  90%
        2-log                                  99%
        3-log                                99.9%
        5-log                              99.999%
      6.5-log                           99.999968%
        7-log                            99.99999%

The guideline's performance-standard discussion (page 18) states, in
summary:

-   Cooked poultry products: at least a **7-log reduction of
    Salmonella**, or an accepted alternative lethality.
-   Roast, cooked, and corned beef: **6.5-log reduction of Salmonella**,
    or an accepted alternative lethality.
-   Cooked uncured meat patties: the applicable time-temperature
    combinations in 9 CFR 318.23, which achieve a 5-log reduction of
    Salmonella and other relevant pathogens including STEC.

For cooked meat products not subject to a specific performance standard,
FSIS recommends a 6.5-log or 5-log Salmonella reduction target. A 5-log
target requires additional support as described on pages 57--59.

**Do not assume that every meat product can use the same target or
table.** Product category and applicable regulatory requirements matter.

## 4. Come-up time (CUT)

FSIS defines come-up time as the amount of time the product's internal
temperature is between **50°F and 130°F (10°C and 54.4°C) while
heating**.

The guideline describes a Come-Up-Time Option under which the total time
the product temperature is between 50°F and 130°F is **6 hours or less**
(page 24). FSIS explains that this option is intended to control
*Staphylococcus aureus* growth to no more than 2-log and prevent
enterotoxin formation.

Important qualifications from the guideline:

-   The temperatures refer to the product's internal temperature.
-   For intact products, surface-temperature monitoring during CUT may
    be used only when the establishment supports that the product is
    intact and pathogens have not been introduced below the surface.
-   For non-intact products---such as mechanically tenderized, injected,
    or vacuum-tumbled products---the temperature should be measured
    internally at the centre.
-   The CUT option described here applies to products cooked to
    lethality. Partially cooked products that do not receive full
    lethality require consideration of the FSIS Stabilization Guideline.
-   If a product has not yet achieved a lethal time-temperature
    combination, the CUT does not simply restart. The guideline
    discusses a reset when a product has achieved lethality and is then
    cooked again.

FSIS acknowledges that some products, such as ham or beef brisket, may
not be able to meet the six-hour option because of their heating
characteristics. Long-CUT processes are identified as a scientific gap
unless supported by appropriate additional scientific information.

**Practical distinction:** CUT is not the same as the dwell time in
Table 2. CUT concerns the heating period in the 50--130°F
internal-temperature range; dwell time concerns the required hold after
the product reaches a listed endpoint temperature.

## 5. Relative humidity: why sous vide is treated differently

For many conventional cooking processes that use moving hot air or
steam, FSIS treats relative humidity as a critical operating parameter.
A dry product surface can make pathogens more heat tolerant and can
undermine the lethality assumptions behind the time-temperature tables.

However, FSIS expressly identifies sealed, moisture-impermeable bags and
complete immersion in liquid throughout cooking as situations in which
moisture is inherently maintained (page 31).

The guideline's four humidity options (page 26) are relevant to
processes that need to address humidity:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Option                              FSIS description
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Option 1                            Maintain oven humidity by
                                      continuously introducing steam for
                                      50% of cooking time, or 1 hour,
                                      whichever is longer.

  Option 2                            Maintain oven humidity using a
                                      sealed oven for at least 50% of
                                      total cooking time, or 1 hour,
                                      whichever is longer.

  Option 3                            Maintain relative humidity at 90%
                                      or above for at least 25% of total
                                      cooking time, or 1 hour, whichever
                                      is longer.

  Option 4                            Maintain relative humidity at 90%
                                      for the entire cooking time.
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Options 1 and 2 are available for endpoint time-temperature combinations
of at least 145°F plus the required dwell time. For endpoint
temperatures below 145°F, the guideline directs establishments to Option
3 or 4, depending on total cooking time.

These oven-humidity options should **not** be mistaken for extra
requirements imposed on a qualifying sealed-bag water-bath sous vide
process.

## 6. Meat versus poultry

FSIS provides separate endpoint tables:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Product                 FSIS table              General lethality
                                                  target described in the
                                                  guideline
  ----------------------- ----------------------- -----------------------
  Meat products           Table 2 (page 35)       6.5-log or 7-log, as
                                                  applicable

  Chicken products        Table 3 (page 37)       7-log

  Turkey products         Table 4 (page 38)       7-log
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

The poultry tables account for fat content. The guideline also explains
that Salmonella heat tolerance can differ by poultry species. For
poultry species other than chicken or turkey, or mixtures of poultry
species, FSIS recommends selecting an endpoint temperature and using the
longer dwell time from the chicken or turkey table for the relevant fat
content (page 36).

Products that mix poultry and meat must achieve a 7-log reduction of
Salmonella, as discussed in the guideline.

The meat Table 2 should therefore not be substituted for the poultry
tables when cooking poultry.

## 7. Other points relevant to process safety

### Temperature measurement and limits

The guideline discusses establishing critical limits that account for
measurement-device accuracy (page 20). A process must be monitored in a
way that demonstrates that its critical parameters are consistently met.
The document's HACCP validation discussion is intended for
establishments; it is not a blanket instruction that every home cook
must use a particular instrument tolerance.

### Fat content

FSIS notes that fat can increase the heat tolerance of some
microorganisms ("fat protection"). The poultry tables therefore
distinguish fat levels, and product composition matters when selecting a
supported process.

### Package integrity

A sealed cook-in-bag process depends on the bag remaining intact and
containing moisture. The guideline's discussion of dried products cooked
under moist conditions (pages 90--91) specifically emphasizes complete
sealing, package integrity, and the risk of compromised bags. That
section concerns a particular scientific gap involving dried products;
it should not be generalized as a separate validation rule for every
ordinary sous vide preparation.

### Cooling, storage, and post-cooking handling

This cooking guideline focuses on lethality during cooking. It does
**not** provide a complete set of cooling and refrigerated-storage rules
for every sous vide product.

FSIS treats stabilization as a separate food-safety issue, including
control of spore-forming pathogens such as:

-   *Clostridium perfringens*
-   *Clostridium botulinum*

For cooked products that will be cooled, stored, or handled as
ready-to-eat foods, consult the separate **FSIS Stabilization Guideline
for Meat and Poultry Products** and the requirements applicable to the
product and process. Meeting a Table 2 cooking combination does not, by
itself, establish that subsequent cooling and storage are safe.

## 8. Applicability and limitations of the guideline

The 2021 FSIS Cooking Guideline is intended to support establishments in
designing and validating HACCP cooking processes. It is not a
general-purpose recipe book or a complete household food-safety
standard.

The document states that it does not have the force and effect of law.
Applicable regulations and product-specific requirements remain
relevant.

The guideline also identifies products and processes outside its scope
or requiring additional scientific support. A process should not be
assumed to be covered merely because it involves heating meat in a bag.

For commercial use, the establishment's actual process must match the
critical operating parameters in the scientific support and be
appropriately validated and monitored.

## 9. Sous vide-focused summary

The most relevant points for a conventional sealed-bag, water-bath sous
vide process are:

1.  **Humidity:** FSIS identifies cooking in a sealed,
    moisture-impermeable bag and complete immersion in liquid throughout
    cooking as situations where relative humidity does not need to be
    addressed separately.
2.  **Internal temperature:** The applicable time-temperature
    combination must be achieved in all parts of the product.
3.  **Dwell time:** The required hold begins when the product reaches
    the selected internal endpoint temperature; it is not the same as
    total bath time.
4.  **Come-up time:** The guideline describes a 6-hour limit for the
    total time product temperature is between 50°F and 130°F under its
    CUT option, subject to the qualifications in the guideline.
5.  **Product category:** Use the appropriate meat or poultry table and
    applicable lethality target.
6.  **After cooking:** Cooling, storage, and handling need separate
    food-safety controls.
